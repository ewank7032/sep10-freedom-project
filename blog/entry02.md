## What Software & Hardware I Found

With some researching, I found some software that can be used to educate people on nuclear reactors. I originally wanted to look at software people would use daily at their job in this industry, but those were too restricted to have much detail on them, so I chose software that were for education instead. For hardware, since I'm also trying to pitch the safety of nuclear power to people, I chose to include details about protection systems used in a nuclear reactor. When I was searching for software, I first searched "nuclear reactor simulation", and many variations of that search to find a page with sufficient detail on training software, but I eventually opted for "nuclear reactor 3D model" and found most of what I had listed. For hardware, I knew it from the top of my head, so I searched "reactor protection system" and got two Wikipedia articles about it.

## Sources

[Energy Encyclopedia](https://www.energyencyclopedia.com/en/online-3d-models) was a great place for some educational software related to my topic. It contained 3D models in good detail of the two most common nuclear reactor types in the industry, the [Pressurized Water Reactor](https://3d.energyencyclopedia.com/npp-pwr/) and the [Boiling Water Reactor](https://3d.energyencyclopedia.com/npp-bwr). Theres also way more about future software that I could use later. [This simulation](https://dalton-nrs.manchester.ac.uk/) by the University of Manchester is also great. There isn't much to operate, but its the basics. For hardware, Wikipedia had the most straight-forward [articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiling_water_reactor_safety_systems#Reactor_Protection_System_(RPS)), while [this source](https://nrcoe.inl.gov/publicdocs/SystemStudies/rps-bw-description.pdf) was more detailed, but I had to dig through it to find specific information.
