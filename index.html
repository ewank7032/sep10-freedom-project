The Nuclear Energy Industry

Clean, reliable energy plagued by stigma.

Why Nuclear?

Nuclear energy is one of the cleanest and highest energy producing energy sources. It emits essentially no air pollution during normal operation, while also being able to reliably operate continously at high power. However, many misconceptions surround nuclear energy, leading people to fear this energy source. This website aims to clear up those misconceptions.

How Nuclear Reactors Work

Before learning how nuclear reactors are safe, you probably need to know how nuclear reactors work. Nuclear reactors also just involves boiling water somewhere and then uses the steam to turn a turbine generator for power. Perhaps knowing it just involves boiling water clears something up for you. We'll really only get into detail on how it boils the water.

Without diving into anything too specific, nuclear reactors essentially just has uranium fuel rods that go through nuclear fission chain reactions to produce heat. Control rods help reduce how much heat they can release by limiting the reactions, and moderators help sustain the nuclear reaction. All of this is contained in a vessel that can handle high pressures. The Pressurized Water Reactors (PWRs), and Boiling Water Reactors (BWRs), are the two most common reactor types. For PWRs the pressure in the vessel is high to the point water cannot boil, while BWRs, boil water inside the vessel. The image on the right is a PWR (not to scale).

What happened at Chernobyl?

On April 26, 1986, the Chernobyl disaster occurred, where unit 4 in the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant of Soviet Ukraine exploded, and radioactive particles were released all over the region and spread to Europe through winds. This massively lowered public trust of nuclear power, especially in Germany, as anti-nuclear movements have already been active since the 70s.

However, it is important to understand that this is not a reflection of all nuclear power plants' safety. Chernobyl's reactor design was unstable due to cost cutting, workers were pressured into ignoring safety procedures, and the computer warning the operators to shut off the reactor was simply turned off.
